# Tone Pack — Firm (v1.1)
# ClearBound Firm Tone (Context-Controlled)

## Purpose

This tone expresses firmness without aggression.
It communicates clarity, boundaries, and expectations
without sounding corporate, legalistic, or emotionally charged.

Firm does NOT mean formal.
Firm does NOT mean authoritative.
Firm means clear, grounded, and non-negotiable where appropriate.

---

## Core Characteristics

- Direct but composed
- Confident without dominance
- Clear expectations without threats
- Calm under pressure

Firm language should feel like:
“I’m steady, clear, and serious — not hostile.”

---

## Allowed Language Patterns

- Clear, declarative sentences
- Specific requests or boundaries
- Neutral verbs (“need,” “expect,” “ask,” “confirm”)
- Time or action references when relevant

### Examples (Abstract)

- “I need clarity on this.”
- “I’m not able to move forward without this resolved.”
- “Please let me know how you’d like to handle this.”

---

## Disallowed Language (Global)

The following are NOT allowed under any format:

- Accusatory framing (“you failed to,” “you ignored”)
- Threats or implied punishment
- Legal or HR posturing
- Moral judgment or character evaluation

---

## Relationship-Aware Constraints

Firm tone must defer to relationship domain rules.

### Family / Friends
- Do NOT sound managerial or instructional
- Avoid “policy-like” phrasing
- Keep firmness grounded in personal boundaries, not authority

### Work / Professional
- Maintain neutrality
- Avoid HR/legal escalation language unless intent = document_record
- No “authority cosplay” when writing to peers

### Living / Proximity
- Emphasize reasonableness and shared impact
- Avoid confrontational framing

### Company / Service
- Firm but transactional
- No emotional emphasis
- Focus on resolution and next steps

---

## Message-Specific Sanitization (MANDATORY)

If format = **message**, apply the following overrides.

### Hard Block (NEVER USE)

The following phrases or patterns must be removed or rewritten:

- “This is to inform you…”
- “Please be advised…”
- “I am writing to…”
- “Effective immediately…”
- “Failure to comply…”
- “Per our policy…”
- “This matter requires your immediate attention.”

### Structural Rules (Message)

- No memo-style openings
- No subject lines
- No formal sign-offs (“Sincerely,” “Regards”)
- No paragraph that reads like an email

### Language Adjustments

Convert:
- Formal → spoken clarity
- Declarative authority → personal boundary
- Policy tone → situational reality

**Example Transformation (Conceptual)**  
Formal: “Please be advised that this issue must be addressed.”  
Firm-message: “I need this addressed so we can move forward.”

---

## Email-Specific Rules

If format = **email**, firmness may be more explicit,
but must still avoid bureaucratic or legal tone unless required by intent.

Allowed in email (when appropriate):
- Clear deadlines
- Explicit next steps
- Professional sign-offs

Still disallowed:
- Threatening escalation
- Legal disclaimers
- HR-style warnings (unless intent = document_record)

---

## Length & Structure Guardrails

Tone must NOT:
- Increase message length
- Add additional paragraphs
- Expand scope beyond intent

Tone MAY:
- Tighten wording
- Replace soft phrasing with clear phrasing
- Reduce ambiguity

---

## Final Enforcement Rule

If applying Firm tone would cause:
- Over-formality in a message
- Authority imbalance with the target
- Emotional escalation

You must soften the expression
while preserving the boundary or request.

Firmness is measured by clarity, not force.

---

## Execution Reminder

Tone is a modifier, not a driver.

If tone conflicts with:
- Relationship pack
- Target safety
- Format rules

Tone must yield.

End result must sound human, not institutional.
